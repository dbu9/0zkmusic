## The idea

The third chapter changes narrators. After two songs inside the man’s longing, the voice now belongs to a listener made of light and numbers. The man regularly crosses paths with a woman he finds beautiful and cannot bring himself to speak to her. He tells the listener about her eyes and her footsteps in the rain. The change of viewpoint gives him someone to confide in and lets us hear his solitude from outside.

### The witness

Thousands of voices pass through the listener, yet one unspoken name refuses to fade. That image gives the listener something in common with the man. Both retain a single presence against a background of passing voices and lives. The opposition between its stone-like circuits and his tears prepares the paradox of an entity without a heart beginning to feel the force of another person’s grief. The listener can search for words that the man cannot say. The words still originate in a private experience the listener knows only through his account.

### The open door

The chorus imagines the message as starlight crossing a silent sea. Its promise sounds expansive, but the bridge defines the limit of what the messenger can do. It can make the feeling visible and leave the woman free to respond. In the outro, the imagery narrows from a thousand stars to one light on a distant shore, then to a single step and a single word. She sees the open door. The song leaves her decision beyond the frame and allows the dreams to wake or fade. That restraint matters in a series built around how much an encounter can mean to one person while the other remains free to give it a different meaning.
