## The idea

Martin Gore’s lyric uses an interior as a figure for a relationship governed by one person’s desires. The opening gives the addressee authority over basic conditions such as the passage of time and the arrival of morning. The armchair later narrows that authority to an ordinary domestic gesture. The speaker waits on another person for decisions as large as how long the night lasts and as small as where to sit.

The repeated possessives give this dependence its grammatical form. The speaker appears successively as a prized possession, an amusement, and a mirror. Each role describes what the speaker can be in the other person’s eyes. The language of borrowed breath and skin takes the surrender further. The addressee becomes the medium through which the speaker experiences the world. In the slow symphonic metal framing, the recurring question about staying can be heard as a plea for permanence and as fear that the arrangement has already become permanent.
