## The idea

The shore is already far behind when the song opens, and the water knows the speaker’s name. That small reversal makes the sea feel uncannily familiar. Home is where the speaker ought to go before the stars disappear, yet the place where daylight cannot reach is the one that seems to recognize them. Even the shadow moving below the surface is described as a light.

As the speaker goes deeper, quiet becomes something felt inside the body. The signal from land is gone, and the speaker can no longer name any place they’re running from. That makes “Take me under” sound less like a cry made in panic with every repetition. By the time they ask for every compass to fail, they’re giving up the means of turning back. The track’s 160 BPM motion gives that decision an urgency the voice itself has begun to shed.
