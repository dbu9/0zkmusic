## The idea

“The Silence” returns to the coffin from “The Voice Beneath” after the voice appears to have gone quiet. The man lays his ear against the wood and hears what he once asked for. He even credits the feeling with having kept its word. That courtesy is poignant because the agreement was always between him and something inside himself. He now has to discover how much of his inner life was organized around listening for an answer.

### The door that has become an outline

He tries speaking the notes he remembers. Their cold return suggests recollection stripped of the sensation that originally made it matter. He can still trace the outline of a door, but the way through it has vanished. The burial metaphor makes that loss physical. Pressing his ear harder can bring him closer to the wood and still leave him at the same distance from what he wants to hear.

### The peace he asked for

The commands in the chorus still contradict each other, though now they go unanswered. The man asks for the feeling to stay buried and to speak, then asks it to leave him neither with his peace nor with the old pain. He has gained an interval of freedom and finds that absence has a weight of its own. The final image leaves him beside the coffin after the light has gone. The voice’s silence is real within the scene, and his inability to rise leaves open what a fresh encounter with the woman might awaken.
