## The idea

The opening movement of Beethoven’s sonata establishes an independent pattern of motion beneath its melody. The triplet figures keep circulating while the upper voice moves in longer, sparsely spaced gestures. The tension between those two rates of motion accounts for much of the movement’s suspended character. A change of harmony can feel consequential even when the prevailing texture barely changes.

That principle makes the source a natural point of departure for trance. The listener can remain oriented by a recurring figure while small changes above and around it determine the emotional direction. The cover casts a solitary figure beneath a moon that dominates the landscape. As an image for the arrangement, it reflects the difference in scale between the single melodic line and the space that repetition makes available around it.
