Once, the world  
waited behind every morning.  

Every door  
opened into another.  
Every mistake  
still had another year.  

I said: later.  

And later  
was a country  
without borders.  

Now I can see  
the first walls  
on the horizon.  

Not because I’m tired.  
Not because I surrender.  

Some roads  
simply no longer exist.  

I could go back —  

but I could never return.  

The street is still there.  
The house.  
Maybe even the light.  

But the man  
who might have lived there  
is gone.  

No grave.  
No name.  
No memory.  

Only a life  
that never learned  
to breathe.  

And suddenly  
I understand:  

Time does not pass.  

It closes.  

One door.  
Then another.  
Then another.  

Quietly.  

Forever.  

We call it future  
while there is still enough of it.  

We call it fate  
when the roads begin to disappear.  

And one day  
only one remains:  

beneath black stars,  
with all my unlived lives  
walking behind me.  

I do not mourn  
yesterday.  

I mourn  
the tomorrow  
that yesterday  
still allowed.  

Someday —  

I thought  
you were a place.  

But you were only  
another name  

for the time  

I still had.
