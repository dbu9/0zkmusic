## The idea

The song is an elegy for a future that once seemed available. Its opening gives time the features of a landscape. Mornings open like doors, “later” has the breadth of a country, and a mistake can still be absorbed by another year. When walls appear on the horizon, the metaphor makes a change in the speaker’s sense of possibility visible as a change in the lay of the land.

The surviving street and house sharpen that loss. A person can go back to an address and still be unable to return to the life they might have led there. The threefold repetition of “No” gives that unrealized self a kind of funeral litany. Later, the image of unlived lives walking behind the speaker makes their absence felt as a presence. These are possibilities with enough emotional weight to haunt the one life that took shape.

Toward the end, the isolated phrases enact the closure they describe. Door after door falls on its own beat, and “Someday” receives a final address as though it were a place or a person the speaker could still reach. The title names the object of grief with particular precision. Yesterday is the last vantage point from which that other tomorrow was still possible.
