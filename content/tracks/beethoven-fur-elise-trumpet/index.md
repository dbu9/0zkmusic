## The idea

Beethoven’s bagatelle begins with a narrow oscillation between two adjacent notes before the melody descends and opens out. That hesitant gesture recurs after contrasting episodes, bringing the piece repeatedly back to a recognizable point. A useful way to hear the form is as a series of returns to an unfinished thought. The intervening passages alter the character of the return even though the opening figure remains familiar.

The choice of trumpet shifts attention toward articulation and breath. A pianist can let the opening notes flicker under the fingers. A trumpet player has to give each recurrence a shape within a breath, making the small delays and arrivals of the phrase especially prominent. The cover’s distant figure beneath dark arches offers a visual counterpart to that premise. One small gesture can retain its intimacy even when it has an expansive space in which to resonate.
