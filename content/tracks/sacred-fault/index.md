## The idea

“Sacred Fault” turns confession into intimacy. Purity and blindness belong to the past; darkness is no longer an outside force but something shared, held, and even blessed. The speaker asks for neither innocence nor release—only to be received completely, shadow and shame included.

That tension gives the track its devotional charge. Falling sounds like prayer, surrender like peace, and the beloved occupies the dangerous space between witness and divinity. Against a Berlin-trance pulse, the short lines become invocations: name, flame, silence, call. The fault is sacred because it is where the divided self finally becomes whole enough to be seen.
