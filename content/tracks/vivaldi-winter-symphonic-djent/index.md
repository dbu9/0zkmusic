## The idea

The sonnet accompanying Vivaldi’s “Winter” treats weather as something registered by the body. Stamping feet and chattering teeth give way to the careful steps of someone crossing ice, then to a fall and a sudden flight. That sequence gives the concerto a physical drama. Rhythm conveys both the need to move and the danger of putting weight in the wrong place. Djent’s sharply articulated accents are well suited to bringing out that aspect of the source. [Library of Congress notes on The Four Seasons](https://lcweb2.loc.gov/static/programs/national-recording-preservation-board/documents/The-Four-Seasons_Furuya.pdf)

The original slow movement locates the listener beside a fire while the rain goes on outside. Its warmth depends in part on a change to major. The experiments with moving major-key passages into minor raise a question about what this shelter would mean in a darker arrangement. The fireside scene establishes that winter can offer pleasure as well as danger, and that temporary safety makes the return to the ice consequential.
