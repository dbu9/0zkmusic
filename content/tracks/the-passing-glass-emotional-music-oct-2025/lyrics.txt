I see the trees — they wave goodbye,  
their branches fade against the sky.  
The train moves on, the hours bend,  
like love that burned, but couldn’t mend.  

Your face dissolves in glass and rain,  
a fleeting ghost that speaks my pain.  
The fields blur out, the rivers gleam,  
and time runs backward in a dream.  

Each mile repeats what hearts once knew —  
the warmth of me, the ache of you.  
The sun retreats, the shadows swell,  
the wheels hum low — a kind of knell.  

I watch the past in silver streams,  
the moving world, the dying dreams.  
And though I know you’re gone for good,  
I’d hold you still — if only I could.
