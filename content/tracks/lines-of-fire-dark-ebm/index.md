## The idea

“I speak in broken glass” gives the voice a texture before it gives us a story. The shards might cut the person hearing the words or the mouth that has to say them. The next verse makes the exchange painfully plain. One person asks for love and then for silence, and the speaker answers both requests with noise. The repeated “you wanted” sounds like an admission, though the speaker takes enough pride in the thunder to make that admission uneasy.

Once the city starts to echo the speaker, a private quarrel has an audience. The beloved’s name becomes a scream, something everyone might hear even as the person it names recedes from the song. By the last verse the speaker calls themself the verse and the explosive kiss. It’s a boast with a cost. Having made an identity out of the damage their words can do, what could this poet say to the person who asked for love?
