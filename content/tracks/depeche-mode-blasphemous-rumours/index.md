## The idea

The lyric stages a crisis of faith through two episodes of suffering and the language a family has inherited for explaining them. The first verse treats a sixteen-year-old’s survival after a suicide attempt as a mercy. The second follows a young woman who discovers faith and delight in life before a chance collision proves fatal. The arrangement of the verses makes it increasingly difficult to fit survival or death into the logic of reward and punishment.

The mother’s gestures give the argument its human scale. The candles survive in memory, prayer gives her somewhere to take her guilt, and the birds continue singing on the day she loses a daughter. Against these observations, the chorus imagines divine laughter. Its opening disclaimer is ironic. The speaker still observes the etiquette of belief even as grief compels them to utter what that etiquette forbids. The symphonic djent treatment suits an accusation addressed to heaven, while the recurring image of the mother’s tears keeps the song anchored in the experience that prompted it.
