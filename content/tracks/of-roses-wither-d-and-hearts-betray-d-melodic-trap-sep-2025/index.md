## The idea

Early-modern diction—crown’d, doth, thy, lute mute—casts betrayal as a small Renaissance tragedy under melodic trap’s dark, nocturnal weight. Soul crowned then cast away; kiss that burns and will not stay; name written in stars that now laugh.

Heart as glass in shards; cup of bitter rain; wound beyond god or time. The archaism might be costume or genuine yearning for a language large enough for ruin. Either way, the feeling is contemporary: love as grave, morning that rises for everyone else.
