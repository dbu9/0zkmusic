## The idea

The adaptation turns the arrival of the sun into a collective reckoning. It opens with people waiting for light and with an equivocal command about fear. The German imagery gives the light the actions of a violent agent. It speaks in tongues of fire, erupts from fists, and leaves marks that survive the places where they were made. The sun comes to stand for a force welcomed in expectation and understood only through the damage it leaves.

The count gives that upheaval an orderly form. At first the world counts silently. Later the Earth itself counts aloud, after the stars have begun to disappear. Personifying the Earth brings the whole world inside the ritual. The lyric moves in the same passage from a mark on one chest to the extinction of distant lights, giving the loss of a single heart and the collapse of the sky a common scale. The trumpet and symphonic metal framing are apt for this reading of the count as a ceremonial announcement of something nobody can stop.
