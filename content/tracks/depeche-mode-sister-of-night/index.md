## The idea

At first the speaker describes the woman’s longing in images of fire and hunger, as though desire has a voice and can make demands of the body. The later verses give that figurative intensity a public setting. A woman crosses the light in a dress that the speaker calls sad. The transferred epithet lets a garment carry the unhappiness she is trying to conceal, even as she hopes to be seen.

Daylight threatens to expose the effort involved in appearing desirable. The chorus complicates the speaker’s position by bringing their own trembling and need for reassurance into view. Addressing her as a sister gives their desire a language of kinship. The person describing her insecurity is also asking her to provide comfort. The heaviness of the djent arrangement suits the recurring weight of that hunger, while the appeal at the center of the lyric remains tentative and exposed.
